EMKAY

A stock OpenVoiceOS voice assistant, running in your browser.

Open Emkay

Early alpha · free and open source · nothing to install

What it is

Emkay is OpenVoiceOS – the open, privacy-minded voice assistant – running entirely inside a web page, with the Mycroft Mark II face as its screen. A phone, a tablet, a TV or a Raspberry Pi in kiosk mode becomes an assistant without an app store, sideloading or setup on the command line.

"Emkay" is "MK" said out loud – a nod to Mycroft's Mark I and Mark II.

Get started

  1. Open app.emkay.ai. The first start downloads OpenVoiceOS and takes a few minutes; later starts are quicker.
  2. Tap the screen when it says so – browsers only allow sound and the microphone after a tap.
  3. Choose Set up to pick language, voice and more, or start with the defaults (your phone's language and OpenVoiceOS' public speech servers).
  4. Tap the face to talk, or ⌨ to type. Everything else is under ⚙.
  5. To keep it on the home screen: the browser's menu → Add to Home screen (Android) or Share → Add to Home Screen (iPhone).

What works today

  • OVOS core with the installer's default skills, the homescreen and the Mark II face.
  • Speech in and out through OpenVoiceOS' public servers.
  • The stock OVOS control panel: settings, setup wizard, installing skills and plugins.
  • The phone as hardware: volume, location, power, sensors (battery, network, screen, motion, light) and the camera.
  • Settings stay on the device and can be saved to and restored from a file.

Languages: English, Danish, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Galician, Catalan, Basque, Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil) and Dutch.

Privacy

OpenVoiceOS runs in your browser, on your device. With the default setup, what you say is sent to OpenVoiceOS' public speech-to-text and text-to-speech servers; the control panel lets you choose others. Settings live in the browser's own storage on the device.
